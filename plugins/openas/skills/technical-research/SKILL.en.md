# Technical research

Identify the question, scenario and decision supported. Reuse [project context](../../references/project-context.en.md) and existing findings without repeated interviews. Split into answerable subquestions and identify required documents, source, data or experiments, with investigation bounds and stop conditions.

Prefer official documentation, specifications, original papers, releases and actual source. Verify volatile versions, prices, license terms and maintenance with dated current sources; use available search when requested. Record title, URL/path, version/commit, access date and the specific supported claim. Secondary sources are leads; search snippets are not full evidence.

Separate facts, inferences, assumptions and unknowns. Resolve conflicts by time, version, scenario and measurement definition, not cherry-picking. Without retrieval analyze provided material and disclose freshness gaps; do not invent references. Compare only decision-relevant applicability, limits, integration and evidence strength. Features do not imply unmeasured performance.

Where literature cannot resolve a critical unknown, define a minimal experiment with hypothesis, input, control, environment/version, metrics, threshold and resource ceiling. Execute only with existing authorization/environment, recording commands/results separately from proposals. Do not automatically install tools, buy services or upload project content.

Deliver conclusions/conditions, evidence comparison, limits/counterexamples, unknowns, next steps and sources. Bound conclusions by actual coverage; failure to find evidence does not prove absence. Use [solution design](../solution-design/SKILL.en.md) for architectural choices. A single fact does not need a long report. Available [Context7](../../integrations/context7/README.en.md) or [GitHub](../../integrations/github/README.en.md) can help; equivalent host tools are sufficient.
