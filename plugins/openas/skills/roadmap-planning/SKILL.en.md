# Roadmap planning

Start from users, problems, current state, confirmed commitments and [project conventions](../../references/project-context.en.md). Existing research/design feeds the roadmap, while unvalidated opportunities remain hypotheses.

Rank by user value, confidence, implementation cost, risks and dependencies, including maintenance, reliability and migration. Explain RICE/MoSCoW inputs and uncertainty when used; without reach/effort data prefer qualitative ranking rather than fabricated precision. Investigate route-changing unknowns with bounded research/prototypes and explicit decisions/stop criteria before promising full implementation.

MVP is the smallest coherent user task and value-validation loop, with inclusions, exclusions, quality floor and success measures. Each phase needs outcome, scope, entry dependencies, acceptance evidence, risks/tradeoffs and actual owner or unassigned status. Separate value measures from engineering checks, including baseline/target/method and unknowns.

Dates require real resources and estimates; otherwise use near-term/later/exploration or dependency order. Distinguish user commitments from suggested estimates. Shared resources may prevent parallel work. Deliver roadmap, rationale, dependencies, deferred work and reevaluation conditions; identify affected phases when evidence/resources change. Send ready phases to [writing plans](../writing-plans/SKILL.en.md) and dependencies to [task orchestration](../task-orchestration/SKILL.en.md). Roadmaps do not mean implementation, deployment authorization or universal readiness.
