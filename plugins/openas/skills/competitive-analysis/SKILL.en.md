# Competitive analysis

Define the supported decision, users, scenario, region/industry and own project scope. Use [project adaptation](../../references/project-context.en.md) to verify existing capabilities; identify product concepts as proposals. Include direct competitors, meaningful alternatives and current workflows, explaining inclusion/exclusion and retaining user-selected candidates.

Compare aligned versions, plans, regions, billing units and test conditions. Useful dimensions include task completion, integration, deployment, experience, price, licensing and maintenance. Separate unknown from not applicable; absent evidence is not evidence of no support.

Each key claim needs source, date, version/plan and evidence type: official assertion, source inspection, hands-on trial or third-party report. Verify volatile information currently using [technical research](../technical-research/SKILL.en.md). Without network access analyze provided material and disclose freshness limits. Performance/experience comparisons need equivalent tasks, inputs, environments and metrics. Marketing cases, stars and downloads do not establish benchmarks, share or satisfaction.

Explain who each difference affects, when, and migration costs. Separate facts, causal inferences and unvalidated needs. Market-size/share/growth claims need dated, compatible data; public features and complaints do not establish representative demand or willingness to pay.

Deliver a sourced matrix, suitability, demonstrated gaps, opportunity hypotheses, validation methods and coverage limits. Use [roadmap planning](../roadmap-planning/SKILL.en.md) for prioritization and [solution design](../solution-design/SKILL.en.md) for solutions. Opportunities are not commitments; research does not authorize registration, purchase, vendor contact or data upload.
